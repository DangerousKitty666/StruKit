ISO 14120 · Guarding

Illustrated introduction to ISO 14120:2015, covering the selection, design and construction of fixed and movable guards against mechanical hazards. This is a public-source overview, not the full standard or a clause-by-clause compliance checklist.

Numbering below is this guide’s section numbering, not ISO clause numbering. The full ISO 14120 document was not supplied.

1 · Fixed enclosure

Use a fixed barrier where routine access is unnecessary. Assess reach through openings, reach over/under, rigidity, impact and foreseeable ejection. Retained fasteners reduce the chance of losing fixings during removal.

Original conceptual illustration; mesh spacing is not a design dimension.

2 · Movable guard and interlock

Frequent access may call for an interlocked movable guard. Prevent hazardous starting while open and stop the hazardous function on opening. If a person could reach the hazard before it has stopped, assess guard locking that retains the guard until the risk has ceased.

ISO 14120 covers the guard; interlocking-device design and selection belong to ISO 14119. Determine the safety-related control requirements separately.

3 · Design and selection checks
  • Start from the machinery risk assessment and intended access tasks.
  • Check barrier strength, secure mounting, foreseeable impacts and ejected material.
  • Account for visibility, maintenance access, cleaning and the operating environment.
  • Avoid introducing sharp edges, trapping, crushing or shearing hazards.
  • Choose openings and stand-off distance together; use ISO 13857 for reach-distance requirements rather than estimating from these sketches.
  • Verify and inspect the installed guarding and its associated protective functions.
4 · Use and maintenance

Isolate and lock off hazardous energy before maintenance or removal of guarding as required by the task. Do not rely on a stop command alone. Check that guards are present, secure and functional before returning the machine to service.
