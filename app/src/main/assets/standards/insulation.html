1 · Plane-wall conduction
P = λAΔT/d
R = d/λ

Steady one-dimensional conduction. P in W; λ in W/(m·K); A in m²; d in m; R in m²K/W.

Worked example

λ = 0.04, A = 10 m², d = 0.1 m and ΔT = 20 K gives 80 W, ignoring surface films.

2 · Layered walls and U-value
Rtotal = Rsi + Σ(dᵢ/λᵢ) + Rse
U = 1/Rtotal
P = UAΔT

Layer resistances add for heat passing through layers in series. Surface resistances depend on orientation and exposure. Thermal bridges require additional treatment.

Worked example

Rtotal = 2.5 m²K/W gives U = 0.4 W/(m²K). A 20 m² wall at 25 K loses 200 W.

3 · Insulation thickness comparison
U = 1/(Rother + d/λ)

For this plot only: λ = 0.04 W/(m·K), Rother = 0.2 m²K/W. This illustrates diminishing reductions in U as thickness rises.

Worked example

100 mm insulation gives U = 0.370; 200 mm gives 0.192 W/(m²K).

4 · Cylindrical insulation
P/L = 2πλΔT / ln(r₂/r₁)

Steady radial conduction through a cylindrical layer. Radii in m; result W/m. Include surface films and other layers for complete pipe loss.

Worked example

r₁ = 0.025 m, r₂ = 0.05 m, λ = 0.04, ΔT = 50 K gives 18.13 W/m through this layer.

5 · Surface convection
P = hA(Ts − Tair)

h in W/(m²K), obtained from the relevant flow and geometry. It is not a material conductivity.

Worked example

h = 8, A = 2 m² and a 15 K surface-air difference gives 240 W.

6 · Thermal radiation
P = εσA(T₁⁴ − T₂⁴)

Kelvin temperatures; σ = 5.670374419×10⁻⁸ W/(m²K⁴). This simple expression assumes a surface seeing a large isothermal enclosure.

Worked example

ε = 0.8, A = 1 m², T₁ = 323.15 K, T₂ = 293.15 K gives about 159.7 W.

7 · Heat exchangers · LMTD
ΔTlm = (ΔT₁−ΔT₂)/ln(ΔT₁/ΔT₂)
P = UAΔTlm

Use terminal temperature differences for the actual flow arrangement. If both are equal, LMTD equals that common difference. Multipass arrangements may need a correction factor.

Worked example

Terminal differences 40 K and 20 K give ΔTlm = 28.85 K.

8 · Cooling insulation and moisture
surface temperature > dew point

Insulation on cold services must control surface temperature and moisture ingress. A vapour-control layer and sealed joints are often essential.

Worked example

A pipe surface at 7°C in 20°C, 50% RH air is below the approximately 9.3°C dew point.
