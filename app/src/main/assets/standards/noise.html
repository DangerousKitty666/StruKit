Noise & sound levels

Typical examples, not measurements of your location. Distance, equipment and duration matter. Perceived loudness varies; pain is not a reliable warning of hearing damage.

Everyday sound chart · dBABar scale: 0–160 dBA. Ranges represent example levels, not safe exposure bands.

Cinema · 74–104 dBA
Can range from moderate dialogue to very loud effects.
Motorcycles / dirt bikes · 80–110 dBA
Loud engine noise; distance and operation change the level.
Loud headphones / concerts · 94–110 dBA
Very loud music; hearing risk grows with exposure.
Sirens · 110–129 dBA
Intense sound; move away from the source.
Fireworks · 140–160 dBA
Extreme impulses; can cause immediate damage.

Exposure level and duration

NIOSH recommends 85 dBA averaged over an eight-hour workday. Each additional 3 dBA halves the corresponding duration. This is a US occupational recommendation, not a personal guarantee or a UK/LT legal limit.

LevelCorresponding duration
85 dBA8 hours
88 dBA4 hours
91 dBA2 hours
94 dBA1 hour
97 dBA30 minutes
100 dBA15 minutes

Repeated exposure adds up. Reduce noise at source, increase distance and limit exposure; use suitable hearing protection where needed. An ordinary phone microphone is not a calibrated noise assessment.

Reading the chart

dBA applies frequency weighting to approximate human hearing sensitivity. A decibel scale is logarithmic: values should not be added as ordinary numbers. The chart describes sound level, not frequency or pitch. Quiet-sounding low-frequency or impulse noise may need a different assessment.
