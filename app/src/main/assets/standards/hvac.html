1 · Air changes and flow
ACH = 3600Q/V
Q = ACH × V/3600

Q in m³/s; room volume V in m³; ACH in h⁻¹. This is a flow-volume ratio, not proof of complete pollutant removal or adequate ventilation.

Worked example

A 180 m³ room at 4 ACH requires 0.2 m³/s = 720 m³/h.

2 · Duct area and velocity
A = Q/v
D = √(4Q/(πv))

Circular duct diameter D in m. Select velocity with pressure loss, noise and available space in mind.

Worked example

For Q = 0.5 m³/s and v = 5 m/s: A = 0.1 m² and equivalent circular D = 357 mm.

3 · Sensible heating/cooling
Pₛ = ρQ cₚ ΔT

Power in W with cₚ in J/(kg·K). Use the correct local air density. Sign identifies heating or cooling.

Worked example

ρ = 1.204, Q = 0.5, cₚ = 1006 and ΔT = 10 K gives 6.056 kW.

4 · Total coil load
Ptotal = ṁdry (hin − hout)

Use kJ/kg dry air and kg dry air/s to obtain kW. Condensing coils change humidity as well as temperature.

Worked example

ṁdry = 0.6 kg/s and enthalpy drop 15 kJ/kg give 9 kW total cooling.

5 · Moisture removal
ṁwater = ṁdry(win − wout)

Humidity ratios in kg water/kg dry air. Multiply kg/s by 3600 for kg/h.

Worked example

At 0.6 kg dry air/s and Δw = 0.003, condensate rate is 6.48 kg/h.

6 · Sensible heat ratio
SHR = Pₛ/Ptotal

Applicable to cooling loads on a consistent basis; latent load is Ptotal − Pₛ.

Worked example

A 10 kW load with 7 kW sensible has SHR = 0.70 and latent load 3 kW.

7 · Fan power and speed changes
Pshaft = QΔp/η
Q ∝ n; Δp ∝ n²; P ∝ n³

Q in m³/s, pressure in Pa. Fan laws assume unchanged geometry and density with similar operating conditions. Motor efficiency adds another loss.

Worked example

0.5 m³/s at 500 Pa and η = 0.6 requires 417 W shaft power.

8 · Heating-water flow
ṁ = P/(cₚΔT)

For water around ordinary heating temperatures, cₚ ≈ 4180 J/(kg·K) is a useful first estimate. Use fluid data for glycol mixtures.

Worked example

A 20 kW duty with 10 K water drop needs 0.478 kg/s, approximately 1.72 m³/h at ρ = 1000.

9 · Load schedule and system selection
Total duty = fabric + ventilation + internal + process loads

Separate sensible and latent loads, diversity, operating hours and outdoor design conditions. Do not sum heating and cooling loads that occur at different design conditions.

Worked example

Keep occupants, lighting, equipment, solar gains and outdoor air as separate schedule lines so assumptions can be reviewed.
