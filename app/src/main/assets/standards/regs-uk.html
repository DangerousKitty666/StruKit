1 · Choose the jurisdiction

The UK has four building-control systems. Planning permission and building-regulations approval are separate matters. Select the jurisdiction before choosing guidance or an approval route.

Non-domesticUse the relevant non-dwelling guidance and occupancy/use assumptions. Check fire, access, sanitary facilities, ventilation and energy provisions.

Approved Documents explain ways of meeting functional requirements. A: structure; B: fire; C: moisture/contaminants; E: sound; F: ventilation; G: sanitation/water; H: drainage; K: protection from falling/impact; L: energy; M: access. 3 · England — higher-risk buildings

For design/construction, test the prescribed height/storey rules: at least 18 m OR at least seven storeys, together with qualifying use. This includes at least two residential units, a hospital or a care home, subject to statutory exclusions and the legal meaning of a building. The occupation regime has a different scope. Do not classify using overall roof height alone.

Building work on higher-risk buildings generally uses the Building Safety Regulator approval route, subject to exceptions. Check the dedicated criteria and process.

4 · Wales

Use Welsh Approved Documents and amendments. Domestic/non-domestic distinctions and the building’s proposed use guide the applicable volume. Welsh fire, energy and other requirements can differ from England; an English detail is not automatically the applicable Welsh solution.

5 · Scotland

Use the Building (Scotland) Regulations and Scottish technical handbooks: domestic for dwellings, non-domestic for other buildings. Their sections address structure, fire, environment, safety, noise, energy and sustainability. A building warrant and completion process apply where required. April 2026 guidance applies subject to its stated application date and transitional provisions.

6 · Northern Ireland

Use the Building Regulations (Northern Ireland) and Department of Finance Technical Booklets. Check the booklet for the relevant function and building use, along with amendments. District council building control administers the local process. Guidance includes structure, fire, moisture, energy, ventilation, access and protection from falling.

7 · Project information to record

Record jurisdiction, use/occupancy, building height/storeys, new work or alteration/change of use, approval route, applicable document edition and transition dates. Keep structure, fire strategy, access, services and energy requirements coordinated. An engineering calculation alone does not establish regulatory approval.
