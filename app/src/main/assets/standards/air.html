Air · properties & psychrometrics

Equations, worked examples & charts. Expand a subject; original references are linked inside.

1 · Dry-air density
ρ = p / (R T)

ρ in kg/m³; absolute p in Pa; T in K; dry-air R = 287.05 J/(kg·K). Use absolute pressure, not gauge. Ideal-gas approximation near ordinary atmospheric conditions.

Worked example

At 20°C and 101325 Pa: ρ = 101325/(287.05 × 293.15) = 1.204 kg/m³.

2 · Mass flow and continuity
ṁ = ρ Q = ρ A v

Mass flow ṁ in kg/s; volume flow Q in m³/s; area A in m²; mean velocity v in m/s. Density changes with temperature and pressure.

Worked example

Q = 0.5 m³/s at ρ = 1.204 gives ṁ = 0.602 kg/s.

3 · Viscosity and Reynolds number
ν = μ/ρ
Re = ρvD/μ = vD/ν

Dynamic viscosity μ in Pa·s; kinematic ν in m²/s. D is pipe diameter or hydraulic diameter. Flow regime thresholds depend on geometry and disturbances.

Worked example

At μ = 1.81×10⁻⁵ Pa·s, ρ = 1.204, v = 4 m/s and D = 0.2 m: ν = 1.50×10⁻⁵ m²/s and Re ≈ 53200.

4 · Heat capacity and sensible energy
Qₕ = m cₚ ΔT
Pₕ = ṁ cₚ ΔT

Qₕ in J, Pₕ in W, cₚ in J/(kg·K). For dry air near room temperature use about 1006; temperature-dependent values are linked.

Worked example

Heating 0.6 kg/s by 15 K needs 9054 W, excluding losses and moisture changes.

5 · Thermal transport
a = λ/(ρcₚ)
Pr = μcₚ/λ

Diffusivity a in m²/s compares heat spreading to thermal storage. Prandtl number is dimensionless. λ is conductivity in W/(m·K).

Worked example

Using λ = 0.0257, ρ = 1.204, cₚ = 1006 and μ = 1.81×10⁻⁵ gives a ≈ 2.12×10⁻⁵ and Pr ≈ 0.709.

6 · Humidity ratio
pᵥ = φ pₛ(T)
w = 0.62198 pᵥ/(p − pᵥ)

φ is relative humidity as a fraction; pₛ is saturation vapour pressure. Use consistent pressure units. w is kg water per kg dry air, not kg per kg moist air.

Worked example

At 20°C, φ = 0.5, pₛ ≈ 2.339 kPa and p = 101.325 kPa, w ≈ 0.00726 kg/kg.

7 · Moist-air enthalpy
h ≈ 1.006t + w(2501 + 1.86t)

h in kJ/kg dry air; t in °C; w in kg/kg dry air. Reference enthalpy is tied to 0°C. Use the same dry-air mass basis in coil calculations.

Worked example

At 20°C and w = 0.00726: h ≈ 38.55 kJ/kg dry air.

8 · Dew point and condensation
pₛ(Tdew) = pᵥ

Find the saturation temperature corresponding to the vapour partial pressure. A surface below this dew point may collect condensation.

Worked example

For the 20°C, 50% RH example, pᵥ ≈ 1.17 kPa gives a dew point around 9.3°C.
