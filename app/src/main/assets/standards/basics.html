1 · SI units and derived quantities
1 N = 1 kg·m/s²
1 Pa = 1 N/m²
1 J = 1 N·m
1 W = 1 J/s

Use coherent units before substitution. A numerical value without its unit cannot be transferred safely between formulas.

Worked example

10 kN divided by 100 mm² equals 100 N/mm² = 100 MPa.

2 · Prefixes and squared units
k = 10³; M = 10⁶; m = 10⁻³; µ = 10⁻⁶

Apply the exponent to the whole length conversion when converting area or volume.

Worked example

1 mm² = 10⁻⁶ m²; 1 mm³ = 10⁻⁹ m³; 1 cm⁴ = 10⁴ mm⁴.

3 · Pressure conversions
1 bar = 100000 Pa
1 MPa = 1 N/mm²

Gauge pressure is relative to ambient; absolute pressure uses vacuum as zero. Gas laws need absolute pressure.

Worked example

2 bar gauge at 1.01325 bar ambient is 3.01325 bar absolute.

4 · Temperature and differences
T[K] = t[°C] + 273.15
t[°F] = 1.8t[°C] + 32

A 1 K difference equals a 1°C difference. Absolute temperature is needed in gas and radiation expressions.

Worked example

20°C = 293.15 K = 68°F; a rise of 20°C is a rise of 36°F.

5 · Areas and volumes
Circle: A = πD²/4
Cylinder: V = πD²L/4
Sphere: V = πD³/6

Use diameter consistently rather than mixing it with radius. Hollow sections require subtracting the inner volume.

Worked example

A 0.1 m diameter × 1 m cylinder has volume 0.007854 m³.

6 · Dimensionless checks
Re = ρvD/μ
Pr = μcₚ/λ
Nu = hD/λ

Each expression must reduce to no physical unit. Correlations apply only within their stated geometry and validity ranges.

Worked example

For Re, units (kg/m³)(m/s)(m)/(kg/(m·s)) cancel completely.

7 · Energy and efficiency
η = useful output / input
Energy = Power × time

Power is a rate; energy accumulates over time. Efficiency is dimensionless and cannot exceed 1 for a simple energy conversion.

Worked example

2 kW for 3 h consumes 6 kWh = 21.6 MJ.

8 · Drawing scale and ratios
Actual length = drawing length × scale denominator

For a 1:n scale. Printing, screenshots and display zoom may alter the scale; use stated dimensions for fabrication.

Worked example

36 mm measured on a true 1:50 drawing represents 1800 mm.

9 · Constants and significant figures
g₀ = 9.80665 m/s²
π ≈ 3.141592654

Keep extra digits during calculation, then round the result to the accuracy supported by inputs. Standard gravity is a defined reference, not a site survey.

Worked example

Using a nominal 6 mm sheet thickness to calculate mass does not justify gram-level precision for a large plate.
